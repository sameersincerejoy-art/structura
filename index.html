Section 05 — Method

Engineering before simulation

Good analysis begins before the solver is opened.

Hover a stage to read what it involves.

01

Understand

Understand the product, operating conditions, requirements and what is going wrong.

02

Define

Turn the problem into measurable questions and a clear analysis objective.

03

Model

Represent the relevant geometry, materials, loads, contacts and constraints.

04

Verify

Check assumptions, numerical behaviour, convergence and sensitivity.

05

Validate

Where appropriate, compare predictions with testing, measurements or other credible reference data.

06

Decide

Turn the technical result into a design change, operating limit, inspection requirement, test plan or engineering conclusion.

Section 06 — Principles

Built around engineering judgement

Five principles that decide how the work is done — and how far the analysis should be taken.

They are the reason the method is chosen around the physics and the decision, rather than around a particular solver.

Meet the team →

P1

Specialist, not generic

We focus on difficult structural and mechanical problems where specialist analysis can add value.

P2

Physics before method

We use nonlinear, dynamic, fatigue, stability or other advanced methods when the physical problem requires them — not simply because they are available.

P3

Analysis connected to reality

Recommendations consider loads, materials, manufacturing, assembly, operating conditions and practical product constraints.

P4

Verification is part of the engineering

Model assumptions, boundary conditions, convergence, sensitivity and validation are considered as part of the work rather than added at the end.

P5

Direct technical involvement

Structura is deliberately specialist. The engineers who understand the problem are directly involved in the analysis and technical discussion.

High-Speed Cam–Follower Redesign

A spring-return cam–follower mechanism was investigated for an increase from 200 to 260 rpm. A reduced-order model predicted contact loss in the original configuration; redesign screening produced calculated nominal and worst-corner contact-force margins of +36.17 N and +16.26 N within a synthetic uncertainty envelope.

Beyond Static Strength

A literature-informed investigation of alternator mounting bracket fatigue, considering engine-induced cyclic stress, local notch severity and surface condition. An integrated redesign produced a calculated equivalent fatigue-demand indicator of 138.7 MPa against an illustrative baseline of 234.7 MPa under declared assumptions.

Section 09 — Evidence

Evidence before confidence

A model can converge perfectly and still represent the wrong physical problem. We make the boundary between assumption and evidence visible.

Assumed input Used to construct a synthetic study or preliminary model.
Calculated result An independently reproducible analytical or reduced-order calculation.
Simulation result A numerical prediction from a defined model.
Measured result Evidence obtained from a physical component, test or operating system.
Validated result A prediction that has been compared against appropriate independent evidence.
Released design A result accepted against the responsible engineering standard, test basis and project requirements.

Confidence increases as independent evidence increases.

Not every project reaches the final rung. Stating where a result actually sits is part of the engineering.
