Capabilities

Engineering analysis for problems that are difficult to answer with routine methods

When a product is failing, a simulation is inconclusive, a design is close to its limit, or an engineering team needs confidence before making a change, the first question is not “Which software should we use?”

It is: what do we need to understand or prove?

Structura Engineering helps manufacturers and engineering teams answer difficult mechanical questions through failure investigation, structural analysis, verification and validation, dynamics, and analysis-led product improvement.

The method is chosen around the physics and the decision — not around a particular solver.

Core engineering capabilities

Six areas of specialist work

Each begins with the engineering question, not the method.

Capability 01

Failure & Root-Cause Engineering

Understand why the product failed before deciding how to fix it.

A broken component contains evidence about what happened. The useful question is not simply where it broke, but why the failure occurred under the actual operating conditions.

We combine engineering evidence, mechanics and simulation to investigate competing explanations and identify the mechanism that needs to be addressed.

We can help with

  • Failure mechanism assessment
  • Structural failure investigation
  • Fatigue and crack initiation assessment
  • Load-path reconstruction
  • Joint and attachment problems
  • Unexpected yielding or deformation
  • Material behaviour assessment
  • Simulation-assisted root-cause analysis
  • Corrective design recommendations
  • Verification planning for the proposed fix

Useful when

A component has failed repeatedly, a redesign has not solved the problem, or the physical evidence and existing analysis do not agree.

Typical outcome: a technically supported explanation of the failure, the important load or design drivers, and a clear basis for deciding what should change.

Capability 02

Advanced Structural Analysis

Use the simplest model that can answer the question — and add complexity only when the physics requires it.

Not every problem needs nonlinear FEA. But some problems cannot be answered reliably with a simple linear model.

We select the analysis approach according to the behaviour that matters: contact, plasticity, large deformation, instability, cyclic loading, impact, vibration or other nonlinear effects.

Analysis areas include

  • Linear and nonlinear structural analysis
  • Large deformation and material nonlinearity
  • Contact and assembly behaviour
  • Static and quasi-static loading
  • Explicit dynamics and impact
  • Buckling and structural stability
  • Fatigue assessment
  • Fracture mechanics and crack-growth assessment
  • Damage tolerance
  • Modal and vibration analysis
  • Multibody dynamics
  • Fluid-structure interaction where the engineering problem requires it

Useful when

Routine calculations cannot explain the observed behaviour, a design operates close to a structural limit, or the physical response involves interactions that simpler methods cannot capture.

Typical outcome: an analysis that answers a defined engineering question rather than simply producing a larger simulation model.

Capability 03

Verification & Validation

Make the analysis useful enough to support a decision.

A model producing a result is not the same as a model producing a trustworthy engineering result.

Verification examines whether the analysis has been set up and solved correctly. Validation asks whether the model represents the real system adequately for its intended use. The level of work required depends on what the model will be used to decide.

This approach is consistent with established computational V&V practice, including ASME's V&V framework for computational solid mechanics.

We can help with

  • Defining what the analysis needs to establish
  • Analysis and modelling strategy
  • Geometry and model checks
  • Boundary-condition review
  • Material-model assessment
  • Mesh and convergence assessment
  • Numerical and physical sanity checks
  • Sensitivity studies
  • Uncertainty assessment where appropriate
  • Comparison with test or other credible reference data
  • Test-to-analysis correlation
  • Independent technical review
  • Engineering interpretation of results

Useful when

A simulation is being challenged in a design review, analysis and test disagree, an internal CAE result needs independent review, or a model is being used for a decision with significant technical consequences.

Typical outcome: a documented understanding of what the model establishes, what it does not establish, and what additional evidence may be required.

Capability 04

Fatigue, Damage Tolerance & Structural Life

Understand how a structure behaves over its actual service life.

A component does not necessarily fail because of its highest single load. Repeated loading, stress concentration, weld details, crack growth and the sequence of operating conditions can determine the real damage accumulation.

We assess the mechanisms that control structural life and help translate them into design, inspection or operating decisions.

We can help with

  • Fatigue assessment
  • Welded-structure fatigue
  • Stress-life and strain-life approaches where appropriate
  • Load-spectrum assessment
  • Crack-growth analysis
  • Damage tolerance
  • Residual strength assessment
  • Inspection and maintenance basis
  • Sensitivity to load, material and flaw assumptions
  • Design changes to improve fatigue life

Useful when

A component develops recurring cracks, service life is shorter than expected, a structure needs an inspection strategy, or a design change needs to be assessed for durability.

Typical outcome: an engineering basis for understanding the damage mechanism and deciding whether the right response is redesign, inspection, operating restriction or further testing.

Capability 05

Dynamics, Vibration & Stability

Find out what is limiting the machine or structure before changing it.

Vibration problems are often not solved by simply making a component stiffer. The real issue may involve excitation, structural modes, damping, operating speed, joint behaviour, tool dynamics or the interaction between several parts of the system.

Similarly, a buckling load from a simplified eigenvalue calculation is not automatically the real stability limit. We investigate the behaviour that controls the operating envelope.

We can help with

  • Modal analysis
  • Frequency-response assessment
  • Resonance investigation
  • Structural dynamics
  • Vibration root-cause analysis
  • Operating-condition assessment
  • Stability and buckling
  • Nonlinear buckling and post-buckling where required
  • Sensitivity to imperfections, restraints and load path
  • Dynamic design improvements
  • Correlation with measured vibration or test data

Useful when

A machine has an unexplained vibration, an operating speed is restricted, a structure is approaching instability, or a calculated natural frequency does not match what is observed in service.

Typical outcome: identification of the physical mechanism limiting performance and an evidence-based basis for deciding what to modify, measure or restrict.

Capability 06

Composite, Material & Product Engineering

Connect material behaviour to the structure and the product.

Material properties cannot always be treated as generic inputs. Composite lay-up, material direction, nonlinear behaviour, temperature, loading rate and available test data can all influence the answer.

We work with material and structural behaviour together, particularly where the material response is important to the engineering decision.

We can help with

  • Composite structural analysis
  • Laminate and sandwich structures
  • Material behaviour assessment
  • Constitutive model selection
  • Material-model development and calibration
  • Failure-criteria assessment
  • Material substitution studies
  • Structural interpretation of material behaviour
  • Mechanical design and redesign
  • Weight reduction
  • Tooling and fixture design
  • Design-for-manufacturing considerations
  • Prototype and design-validation support

Useful when

A material is being changed, a composite structure behaves differently from expectation, an existing product needs redesign, or a structural improvement must remain practical to manufacture.

Typical outcome: a design or material decision supported by an understanding of both structural behaviour and product constraints.

Method

From engineering problem to engineering decision

We do not start with the solver. A strong engineering analysis is a chain of decisions.

01 · UnderstandWe start with the product, operating conditions, available evidence and the problem you are trying to solve.
02 · DefineWe turn the problem into specific engineering questions that can actually be answered.
03 · ModelWe choose an appropriate representation of geometry, materials, loads, contacts and constraints.
04 · VerifyWe check the model, assumptions and numerical behaviour before relying on the result.
05 · ValidateWhere appropriate and practical, predictions are compared with test data, measurements or other credible reference evidence.
06 · DecideThe final objective is not the simulation itself. It is a design decision, failure disposition, operating limit, validation plan, redesign or next engineering action.

Proportionality

The level of analysis should match the decision

Not every engineering question needs a high-fidelity simulation. Sometimes a hand calculation or simplified model is the right answer. Sometimes the problem requires nonlinear FEA, dynamics, fatigue analysis or physical testing.

We choose the level of analysis according to the question, the available evidence and the consequence of the decision.

That is how we avoid both under-analysis and unnecessary complexity.
