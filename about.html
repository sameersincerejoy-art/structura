The team

A team of engineers.
Specialist perspective.

Structura is a team of engineers, not a single practitioner. The group combines advanced academic training with professional experience gained inside established aerospace, automotive and industrial engineering organisations.

Several members of the team hold a Master's degree in Engineering from IIT Bombay. Between them, team members have worked with established manufacturers and suppliers across these sectors. Collectively, that background covers structural analysis, verification and validation, failure analysis, composite structures, material behaviour, and nonlinear and dynamic problems.

Distinction

Team academic and professional background — the education and previous employment described above — is clearly separated from Structura client work. The first is background and training. The second is the work this practice performs for its clients, and only that work is presented as Structura's. Individual client engagements and client names are not published.

The practice

Why small is an advantage

Structura is intentionally small and specialist. That means clients work directly with the engineers responsible for understanding the problem, developing the analysis and interpreting the result.

There is no handover between sales, project management and analysis. The engineers you discuss the problem with are the engineers doing the work — and the ones who sign off on the conclusion.

You talk directly to the engineers who understand the problem.

Methodology

How the work is done

Every engagement follows the same engineering logic, regardless of industry or method.

01

Understand

Understand the product, operating conditions, available evidence and the problem you are trying to solve.

02

Define

Turn the problem into specific engineering questions that can actually be answered.

03

Model

Choose an appropriate representation of geometry, materials, loads, contacts and constraints.

04

Verify

Check the model, assumptions and numerical behaviour before relying on the result.

05

Validate

Where appropriate and practical, compare predictions with test data, measurements or other credible reference evidence.

06

Decide

Turn the technical result into a design change, operating limit, inspection requirement, test plan or engineering conclusion.

Fig. 02 — Engineering question → model → evidence → decision

The objective is not the most complicated model. It is a model and evidence base that are appropriate for the decision.
