Industries

Where We Work

Engineering problems change by industry. The need for sound engineering does not.

Different products bring different loads, materials, duty cycles, manufacturing constraints and validation requirements.

Our work is focused on engineering-intensive products where understanding the mechanical behaviour of a component, structure or assembly is important to making the right decision.

We work with manufacturers, product companies and engineering teams across India on problems involving failure, structural performance, fatigue, stability, vibration, impact, simulation and design improvement.

The common question is usually simple:
What is the product actually doing — and what needs to change?
Industry 01

Industrial Equipment & Machinery

When equipment has to survive the real operating condition

Industrial machinery and equipment often operate under variable loads, repeated duty cycles, complex interfaces and manufacturing constraints that are difficult to capture with simple calculations.

A design may be adequate on paper but still deform unexpectedly, develop cracks, experience excessive vibration or fail after repeated operation.

Fig. I-01 — Machine frame: load introduction, load path and weld detail

What the work can involve

We start by understanding the actual operating condition, load path and failure mechanism. Depending on the question, this may involve structural analysis, nonlinear FEA, fatigue assessment, stability analysis, vibration analysis, failure investigation or correlation with available test or field evidence.

The objective is not simply to produce an analysis result. It is to determine why the equipment is behaving the way it is, whether the design is adequate, and what should change if it is not.

Typical applications
  • Industrial machinery · Process equipment
  • Material handling · Heavy equipment
  • Fabricated structures · Mechanical assemblies
  • Pressure-containing equipment

Industry 02

Automotive & Mobility

Balancing strength, durability, mass and real-world loading

Automotive, commercial-vehicle and off-highway components often have to meet several competing requirements at once. A component needs to be strong enough, durable enough and manufacturable enough — while avoiding unnecessary mass and cost.

The difficulty is often not running a simulation. It is determining whether the loads, constraints and failure mechanisms represented in the model are representative of the product.

Where we can help
  • Fatigue cracking and durability concerns
  • Structural weakness in components or mounts
  • Chassis and structural load paths
  • Excessive deformation
  • Weight reduction without unacceptable loss of performance
  • Impact and transient loading
  • Welded-component fatigue
  • Vibration and dynamic response
  • Design changes requiring technical assessment
  • Review of existing FEA or analysis results

What the work can involve

We can work from existing CAD, FEA, test data, field failures or incomplete engineering information. The analysis may range from focused hand calculations and simplified models to detailed finite-element analysis, fatigue assessment, nonlinear analysis or dynamic investigation.

Where test or service data exists, it can be used to challenge the model and improve confidence in the engineering conclusion.

The result should help answer a practical question: Can the component meet its intended requirement, and if not, what is the most useful change to make?
Typical applications

Automotive components · Chassis and mounts · Commercial vehicles · Off-highway equipment · Structural components · Durability-critical assemblies

Industry 03

Aerospace & Advanced Structures

Engineering analysis where weight, behaviour and evidence all matter

Relevant team experience

Aerospace structures place particular importance on structural behaviour, lightweight design, material behaviour and the quality of engineering evidence supporting an analysis.

Our aerospace capability is informed by professional experience in aerospace structural engineering, including analysis, verification and validation work. We apply that engineering discipline to appropriately scoped problems involving aerospace and advanced structures.

Where we can help
  • Structural analysis of components and assemblies
  • Lightweight structural assessment
  • Composite and multi-material structures
  • Nonlinear structural behaviour
  • Static and fatigue assessment
  • Damage-tolerance analysis
  • Failure investigation
  • Model verification and sensitivity assessment
  • Analysis-to-test correlation
  • Engineering evidence and technical documentation

What the work can involve

The starting point is the engineering question and the evidence available. We establish the relevant loads, constraints, material behaviour and failure modes before selecting the appropriate analysis method.

For simulation-based work, model verification, sensitivity and validation against suitable physical or reference data are considered where applicable. This is particularly important when a simulation result will be used to support a design decision, test programme or technical review.

Typical applications

Aerospace components · UAV structures · Lightweight structures · Composite components · Multi-material assemblies · Advanced mechanical structures · Defence equipment

Industry 04

Heavy Equipment, Mining & Energy

The industry may change. The engineering problem often does not.

Many products do not fit neatly into one sector. Construction equipment, mining machinery, agricultural equipment, energy systems and other engineered products can face the same fundamental questions:

  • Why did it fail?
  • Will it survive the intended loading?
  • Why does the simulation disagree with reality?
  • Where is the actual structural limitation?
  • What should change before we build or test another prototype?
Where we can help
  • Structural failure and root-cause investigation
  • Fatigue and cracking
  • Excessive deformation
  • Buckling and stability
  • Vibration and resonance
  • Impact and dynamic loading
  • Nonlinear structural behaviour
  • Simulation review and verification
  • Design improvement and weight reduction
  • Analysis-led redesign · Test planning and correlation

A problem-first approach

We do not require a project to fit a particular industry category before we can understand it. If the product has a difficult mechanical behaviour, a failure that is not yet explained, or an analysis that is not providing enough confidence for a decision, we can first examine the engineering question and determine what level of work is justified.

That may mean a focused calculation. It may mean a detailed simulation. It may require test data, material information or a physical investigation.

And sometimes the right conclusion is that a more complicated analysis is not necessary.

Typical applications

Mining equipment · Construction machinery · Agricultural equipment · Energy-related mechanical systems · Engineered products · Fabricated structures · Specialist machinery

Common ground

We work where mechanical behaviour matters

The sector determines the loads, materials, operating environment and applicable requirements. But the engineering process remains consistent.

01

Understand

Understand the product, operating condition and problem.

02

Define

Turn the problem into a question that can actually be analysed or tested.

03

Model

Build an appropriate representation of the geometry, materials, loads, contacts and constraints.

04

Verify

Check whether the model and numerical solution are behaving as intended.

05

Validate

Compare predictions with suitable physical evidence, test results or other credible reference data where applicable.

06

Decide

Turn the engineering evidence into a design, investigation or operating decision.

Fig. 02 — Engineering question → model → evidence → decision

A more detailed model is not automatically a better engineering answer.
The right method is the simplest one that can answer the question with sufficient confidence.
